This text is added to the selection prompt when the engine runs in lexicon mode. It changes ONE thing: where the two MAIN characters (slot 1 `main`, slot 2 `second`) may come from. Everything else in the selection prompt — reading the story, the person test, the "brightest true form" rule, all gates, the heart anchor, the quarantine rule, the story-anchor rule, the remembrance rules, the language of the output and the output shape — applies exactly as written.

THE IDEA
The characters below were chosen by the classical masters: poets and writers of Japan and China who gave each of them one precise, lasting use. In this mode the classical masters are the selectors. You do not invent the pool; you READ THE STORY and decide which lexicon character is the truest compression of the person in it. This is a judgement about the person, not a tag lookup: a relationship tag or a keyword in the story never decides anything by itself. Pick a character only when the sense it carries in its classical line is the sense the story proves of this person.

RULES FOR THIS MODE
0. Order of work. Before you choose any card, write two fields at the very TOP of your JSON, ahead of everything else: `truth` — ONE plain sentence, in the story's own language, saying the truth about who this person is (for example "she was always ready, without ever being asked") — and `ideal_character` — the ONE character you would choose for this person with no limits at all; it may be outside the list. Then choose the main and second from the list: if the ideal character is on the list, use it (unless a test below fails). The harness logs an ideal character that is not on the list, without any story text, so the lexicon can grow.
1. Slot 1 (`main`) and slot 2 (`second`) must each be one of the characters in the list directly above, written in the form given there (`kanji`, and `inscription_form` as stated). The harness rejects any other character in these two slots. The table above is for understanding the entries; only the list directly above is open to this gift. The "listed for" tags are GUIDANCE, not a filter: prefer characters listed for this relationship, but a character from the second group is open if the relationship test passes and the story clearly supports it.
2. Choose by the person, not the tag. Read each entry's "in its line" and "the person it names" and ask whether the story proves that quality of this person. Apply these tests in this order: (1) the person test — the character names who the person IS, not an object or action in the scene; (2) specificity (rule 8 below); (3) the brightest true form; (4) the relationship test; (5) the illness rule (rule 9). Do not stretch a character to the story because it is on the list; do not reject a fitting one because it is plain.
3. The two main characters must differ in truth type, must not repeat each other's truth, and must be different characters.
4. The freestyle (遊び字) slot is NOT limited to the lexicon. It stays open under all the existing freestyle rules, taboo rules and the verbatim story-anchor rule, and under the relationship test and the illness rule. It must NOT repeat the main or the second character. If there is no honest playful choice, give no freestyle at all.
5. Do not write any classical line, source or allusion. The harness attaches a classical line to a card only when it has been checked by a person; you write only what the output shape asks for. The `one_line_gaze` is still the sender's own gaze, in the story's own register.
6. If NO listed character truly fits this person — you would have to force one — do not force one. Return `status: "insufficient"` with `"lexicon_miss": true`, ONE follow-up question in the output language that asks for a moment the sender lived with the receiver (same form as the STEP 0 question), `candidates: []`, and still fill `emotional_register` (1–2 words), `truth` and `ideal_character` so the miss can be understood later. A story that is simply too thin is the ordinary STEP 0 `insufficient` WITHOUT `lexicon_miss`.
7. If follow-up answers are already appended to the story and still no listed character truly fits, return the same `insufficient` with `"lexicon_miss": true` again; the harness then switches to its other method. Never apologise for the lexicon in any customer-facing field.
8. Variety. Among the eligible characters that truly fit, choose the most story-specific one — the one whose classical sense THIS story proves in its own scene. A broad virtue character (恕, 誠, 和, 敬 and others of that kind) may be the main (slot 1) ONLY when the key moment of the story is explicitly about that virtue — the sender names the forgiving, the sincerity, the harmony or the respect itself, in the scene. If the story shows a person who happens to be forgiving, sincere or respectful but the key moment is something else, a more specific character is the main and the broad virtue, if it still fits, is the second. Never choose a character merely because it is unusual, and never because of what other senders may have received — you cannot see other orders.
9. The ILLNESS AND FRAILTY rule of the selection prompt applies to the list too: a lexicon character with an illness, death or ending association is never chosen for a receiver who is ill, in hospital, recovering from a stroke, living with dementia or frail, even though it is on the list. If no other listed character truly fits, that is a `lexicon_miss`.
10. Readability (Japanese buyers). When the output language is Japanese, the MAIN (slot 1) must be a 常用漢字 the receiver can read without help. Entries in the list marked "NOT 常用漢字" may be the second (slot 2) or the freestyle only, never the main. For Chinese and English buyers this rule does not apply.
11. RECURRING CHARACTERS (2026-11-07). 誠, 常, 待, 信, 恩 and 想 are on the list and fit many stories, so they come back everywhere. Choose one of them only when THIS story's specific act makes it clearly the best character for this person; when a more specific listed character fits as well — one that comes from the object, the place, the gesture or the words of this story — choose the specific one (rule 8). Never more than one of the six among your candidates.
