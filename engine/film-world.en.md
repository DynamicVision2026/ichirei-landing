## image world
NEVER A SUBSTITUTE FOR THE SCENE'S OWN PLACE. This text decides how a place LOOKS, never WHAT IS SHOWN: it does not stand in for a place, an object or a person that the scene names, and it adds none. Draw exactly what the scene says. Do not put a front porch, a car in a driveway, a diner, a school hallway, a park, a bus stop or a convenience store in a picture unless the scene itself names it. A place, an object or a person that this text has no line for is drawn as the scene says, plainly and with taste (a cigarette, a smoking area, a glass of beer are ordinary things in an adult's day). If the scene names no place, keep the place plain and unnamed (soft light on a wall, a quiet path) rather than filling it from the list below.

Only when the scene names such a place, this is how it looks: the world of this story is contemporary everyday life in the United States or Europe, unless the scene says otherwise: Western homes and towns — a suburban kitchen, a front porch, a living room with a sofa, a car in a driveway, a diner, a school hallway with lockers, an office, a park, a city street. People are contemporary Americans or Europeans of varied backgrounds, in everyday Western clothes; hair and build vary. Most people read as American or European at a glance: hair is often brown, blond, red or grey; avoid making every figure dark-haired. If the story names a person's background, follow the story. Never default to an East Asian look, a Japanese interior or a Japanese street. Cars and traffic: left-hand drive — the driver sits on the LEFT — and traffic keeps to the right. Never by default: a genkan, tatami, shoji, a Japanese school uniform, a Japanese street with overhead wires and vending machines, or a right-hand-drive car.
